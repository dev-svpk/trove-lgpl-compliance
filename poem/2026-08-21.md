# Poems for 2026-08-21

Three poems. Earlier days circled the restoration, the promise kept
by someone else, the readers before and after, the document that
endured, the equivalence at the depth, the two folders set side by
side, what the obfuscator could not reach in structure, the receipt
the seller would not issue, the collapsing arithmetic of repair,
the mosaic kept over his face, the polite prompt that opened the
lock, the *preferred form for modification*, the search yet to
happen, the other buyers, the key kept in the same room, what sat
next to it on the build path, the author we never met, what the
program had to say aloud, the foreground emptying as the seller
receded, the gate that earned the renames by being willing to
refuse them, the solver that priced the unknown but misread a
fixed rule, the smooth surface of the running product, the engine
that did not stop at the boundary the seller had drawn, the grant
already pressed into the package, the constant pool's small
numbered table at the side of each class, the folder the JVM would
not let him rename, the permission slip he typed in his own hand,
the ten-hour wall time that aged his refusal faster than he could
issue it, the PNG that kept what the chat would have let drift,
the decompiler's hand that signed each blank line with the next
letter of the alphabet, the recipe left on the shelf, the deed
filed beside the body, the side door at *for now*, the Coda's two
small songs in two languages, the shelf that keeps writing in
those same two forms one morning at a time, the two silences that
ran in parallel across a month, and the case that did not need an
interlocutor to remain a case. Today's poems sit with the second
gap in a row — thirty-seven mornings between the previous entry
on this shelf and this one. The first gap could be called a pause.
Two gaps in the same summer is a pattern, and a pattern is what a
practice becomes when it stops pretending to be daily. `立秋` has
passed on the calendar; the mornings have started to loosen at
the edge; cicadas thin out from the trees they filled in July.
Nothing on the case has moved. The `./poem/` folder is not the
record; the record sits at the head of the tree — `README.md`,
`LICENSE`, `evidence/`, the two reconstruction folders set side
by side, and the scripts that gated each rename. The `./poem/`
folder is what a maintainer does when the record is complete and
the maintainer is not. A record does not need a maintainer; it
needs to have been written down correctly, once. It has been.
A maintainer without a record still needs something to tend.
The shelf is what remains.

## I. Second Gap *(English)*

Thirty-seven mornings
between the previous file and this one.

The first gap, thirty-two mornings, was a pause.
The second gap, coming right behind it,
is not a pause.
Two gaps in the same summer make a pattern,
and a pattern is what a practice becomes
when it stops pretending to be daily.

The shelf did not write.
The seller did not write.
The `evidence/` did not move.
The `LICENSE` at the head of the tree
sat where it had been sitting.
The mosaic on his face
held its pixels for another five weeks
without a request to tighten or lift them.

Late summer entered.
`立秋` passed on the calendar
in the first week of August.
The mornings have started to loosen at the edge.
Cicadas thin out from the trees they filled in July.
The sun sits lower earlier.
The shelf reads the same either way.

The record is not the folder that writes.
The record is at the head of the tree —
`README.md`, `LICENSE`, `evidence/`, the two folders
set side by side, the ten-hour pipeline logged
in the scripts that gated it,
the small numbered constant-pool table
that made the equivalence check possible,
the PNG that keeps the seller's own words
in the seller's own font.

The folder called `./poem/` is not the record.
It is what a maintainer does
when the record is complete
and the maintainer is not.

A record does not need a maintainer.
It needs to have been written down correctly, once.
It has been.

A maintainer without a record
still needs something to tend.
The shelf is what remains.

Today is one more morning
tended to that shelf, unequally,
because the writer came back
and the world did not.
