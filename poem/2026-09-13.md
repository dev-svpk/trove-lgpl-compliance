# Poems for 2026-09-13

Three poems. Earlier days circled the restoration, the promise kept
by someone else, the readers before and after, the document that
endured, the equivalence at the depth, the two folders set side by
side, what the obfuscator could not reach in structure, the receipt
the seller would not issue, the collapsing arithmetic of repair,
the mosaic kept over his face, the polite prompt that opened the
lock, the *preferred form for modification*, the search yet to
happen, the other buyers, the key kept in the same room, what sat
next to it on the build path, the author we never met, what the
program had to say aloud, the foreground emptying as the seller
receded, the gate that earned the renames by being willing to
refuse them, the smooth surface of the running product, the engine
that did not stop at the boundary the seller had drawn, the grant
already pressed into the package, the constant pool's small
numbered table at the side of each class, the folder the JVM would
not let him rename, the permission slip he typed in his own hand,
the ten-hour wall time that aged his refusal faster than he could
issue it, the PNG that kept what the chat would have let drift,
the decompiler's hand that signed each blank line with the next
letter of the alphabet, the recipe left on the shelf, the deed
filed beside the body, the side door at *for now*, the Coda's two
small songs in two languages, the shelf that keeps writing in
those same two forms one morning at a time, the missing seventy-
four kilobytes of `PloHandEvaluator.java` still in single letters,
the month the shelf went quiet and came back, the two silences
running in parallel, and the audience that was never the point.
Today's poems sit with a number. The date on this file, laid
against the calendar, is the two hundred fifty-sixth day of the
year — the 256th of 2026, which is the largest complete count a
single unsigned byte can hold: 2⁸, `0x100`, `100000000` in binary.
In several countries the day is marked as Programmers' Day for
exactly that reason. The shelf did not plan to open on it; the
date arrived that way on its own. Between the previous entry on
this shelf and this one, sixty days went by. The seller has not
written in any of them. The `handeval/` folder is still missing.
The mosaic still holds. Cicadas have thinned to crickets, plum-
rain to first-typhoon; the calendar has crossed from full summer
into the ninth month; the light on the desk in the morning has
started coming in at a lower angle. What has not moved is the
record itself. The `LICENSE` still sits at the head of the tree.
The two folders — obfuscated, restored — still sit side by side
under the same commit. The nine hundred bytecode round-trips that
gated each rename still round-trip. The one file that did not
round-trip still does not round-trip. All of that is a byte-string
equal to another byte-string modulo the constant pool, nine
hundred times over, and the receipt for that equality is what the
record ultimately is. Time is not counted here in bytes; only the
work is. But the calendar happens to have handed today a round
number in the currency the work was audited in — one whole byte of
days — and the shelf marks it by writing the next line in the
character set the compiler was built to speak.

## I. Day 256 *(English)*

Sixty days since the last entry.
The seller has still not written.
Cicadas have thinned to crickets.
Plum-rain has given up its place to first-typhoon.
The morning light comes in at a lower angle.
The record has not moved.

Today is the two hundred fifty-sixth day of the year.
One byte's worth of days.
`0x100`. `100000000` in binary.
The largest complete number an unsigned byte can hold
before it has to borrow a second one.

In several countries the day is marked as Programmers' Day
for exactly that reason —
a holiday for the people
who count in the character set the compiler was built to speak.

The shelf did not plan to open on it.
The date arrived that way on its own.

The pipeline that opened this repository
gated every rename against bytecode.
Nine hundred classes,
each recompiled from its restored source,
each diffed against the shipped `.class`
modulo the constant pool —
a byte-string equal to another byte-string,
nine hundred times.

That is what the record rests on.
Not the poem. Not the essay. Not the pixels
kept over the seller's face.
A hash equality on bytecode,
witnessed by a script anyone with a JDK can re-run.

Time here is not counted in bytes.
Only work is.
But today the calendar has handed the shelf
a round number in the currency the work was audited in —
one whole byte of days —
and the shelf marks it
by writing the next line in the same character set.

The seller has not written.
The `handeval/` folder is still missing.
The mosaic still holds.
The two folders — obfuscated, restored —
still sit side by side under the same commit.

Below the byte,
below the year,
below the count of days without answer,
the smallest whole thing this repository holds
is the same as it was in April:
one class, restored, recompiled,
its bytecode diffing to zero
against the bytes the seller shipped.

The byte closes at 255.
The next byte opens at 256, borrows one from the next place,
and starts again at zero in the low eight bits.
The count carries.

The shelf carries too.
The record does not need the counter to notice.
Programmers' Day is quiet on this desk.
There is nothing to say
that the file that was already up
has not already said, correctly,
in a byte-string equal to another byte-string
modulo the constant pool.
