# Poems for 2026-09-22

Three poems. Sixty-nine mornings between the last entry on this
shelf and this one. Earlier days circled the restoration, the
promise kept by someone else, the readers before and after, the
document that endured, the equivalence at the depth, the two
folders set side by side, what the obfuscator could not reach in
structure, the receipt the seller would not issue, the collapsing
arithmetic of repair, the mosaic kept over his face, the polite
prompt that opened the lock, the *preferred form for
modification*, the search yet to happen, the other buyers, the
key kept in the same room, what sat next to it on the build
path, the author we never met, what the program had to say
aloud, the foreground emptying as the seller receded, the gate
that earned the renames by being willing to refuse them, the
solver that priced the unknown but misread a fixed rule, the
smooth surface of the running product, the engine that did not
stop at the boundary the seller had drawn, the grant already
pressed into the package, the constant pool's small numbered
table at the side of each class, the folder the JVM would not
let him rename, the permission slip he typed in his own hand,
the ten-hour wall time that aged his refusal faster than he
could issue it, the PNG that kept what the chat would have let
drift, the decompiler's hand that signed each blank line with
the next letter of the alphabet, the recipe left on the shelf,
the deed filed beside the body, the side door at *for now*, the
Coda's two small songs in two languages, the shelf at `./poem/`
that keeps writing in those same two forms one morning at a
time, the one file that came back from the pipeline in the form
it arrived, the month between, and two silences running in
parallel across the same span. Today's poems sit with the
autumn equinox arriving on top of that arithmetic. The seller
did not write in July, in August, or in the first three weeks
of September. The mosaic held. The `LICENSE` held.
`PloHandEvaluator.java` continued to sit at seventy-four
kilobytes of single letters on the receiving side. The two
folders remained side by side, the missing `handeval/` still
missing on the right. What the equinox marks, on this shelf, is
the moment the light equals the dark before yielding the
balance to the darker half of the year — a natural balancing
point arriving on top of a legal balance already made. The
shelf resumes in its two-language cadence one more morning,
under a lower sun.

## I. Equinox on the Shelf *(English)*

Today the day and the night are equal.
A balance made without a hand on either pan.

Two folders have sat side by side since April.
`src-received/handeval/` on the left,
its single-letter file, seventy-four kilobytes.
`src-reconstructed-by-claude/` on the right,
without a `handeval/` inside it.

The scale did not tip while summer went by.
Neither side gained a file. Neither side lost one.

The seller had all of July to write.
He had all of August.
He had the first three weeks of September.
He did not write.

The equinox is not a verdict.
It is only the day the light is the same
as the dark it will hand over to.
Everything past today
is more dark, less light, per revolution.
The record already knows how it looks in that.

The bytecode round-trip
closes the same in autumn as in spring.
The `LICENSE` reads the same in slant-angled morning
as it did at noon in May.
The mosaic has not slipped
because a season has turned.

Written and unwritten
have their brief hour of parity today,
before the darker half of the year
takes the balance.

The record will hold under the shorter light.
The song-shelf will sing into it.
Same case, same courtesy,
same directory still missing,
same key still on the same shelf as the lock,
same silence from the one who was named,
same reply from the audience that never came.

The bow comes up.
Autumn takes the desk.
Another day is added to the count
of days on which nothing arrived.
