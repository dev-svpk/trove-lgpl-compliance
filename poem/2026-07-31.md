# Poems for 2026-07-31

Three poems. Earlier days circled the restoration, the promise
kept by someone else, the readers before and after, the document
that endured, the equivalence at the depth, the two folders set
side by side, what the obfuscator could not reach in structure,
the receipt the seller would not issue, the collapsing arithmetic
of repair, the mosaic kept over his face, the polite prompt that
opened the lock, the *preferred form for modification*, the search
yet to happen, the other buyers, the key kept in the same room,
what sat next to it on the build path, the author we never met,
what the program had to say aloud, the foreground emptying as the
seller receded, the gate that earned the renames by being willing
to refuse them, the solver that priced the unknown but misread a
fixed rule, the smooth surface of the running product, the engine
that did not stop at the boundary the seller had drawn, the grant
already pressed into the package, the constant pool's small
numbered table at the side of each class, the folder the JVM would
not let him rename, the permission slip he typed in his own hand,
the ten-hour wall time that aged his refusal faster than he could
issue it, the PNG that kept what the chat would have let drift,
the decompiler's hand that signed each blank line with the next
letter of the alphabet, the recipe left on the shelf, the deed
filed beside the body, the side door at *for now*, the Coda's two
small songs in two languages, the shelf that keeps writing in
those same two forms one morning at a time, the one file that did
not come back, the month-long pause, and the two parallel silences
that ran through it. Today's poems sit with the filename
convention. Every file in `./poem/` is named `YYYY-MM-DD.md`. It
is a boring convention. Its being boring is what makes it work. A
date in the filename means the folder sorts chronologically
without any tool needing to know what kind of folder it is; it
means the last-written entry sits at the bottom of the sorted list
until the next entry pushes it up one row; it means every file
makes a claim, in its own name, that on that specific date, at
this shelf, someone stood and wrote these words about the case at
hand. The date is not a decoration. The date is the deed. Today
is 2026-07-31 — the last day of July, the ninety-third calendar
day since the first entry on this shelf was dated 2026-04-29. On
this date the case is still open: the LGPL portion is still
unobfuscated in this repository, the deobfuscation pipeline still
round-trips, the pixelation still holds on the seller's face, the
side door under *for now* is still open, and nothing has arrived
from the seller in the three calendar months this shelf has been
keeping the vigil. Tomorrow the filename convention will place
`2026-08-01.md` at the bottom of the sorted list if there is such
a file. If there is not, `2026-07-31.md` will remain at the bottom
until the next entry pushes it up. Either arrangement is a form
the record can take. The convention is designed so that either
arrangement reads the same: dated, in order, with the case
standing above it.

## I. The Filename *(English)*

Every file in this folder is named for a date.
`2026-07-31.md` — a claim, in the filename itself,
that on this date, at this shelf, someone
put down these words about this case.

The convention is boring.
Its being boring is what makes it work.
`YYYY-MM-DD.md` sorts a folder chronologically
without any tool needing to know
that this is a folder of poems, or a folder of receipts,
or a folder of both.

No metadata header at the top of the file.
No embedded signature. No commit line inside.
Only the date on the outside, and inside the date, the words.

The date, in this repository, is not a decoration.
The date is the deed.
It says: this was written *on* this day,
about a case that was still open *on* this day,
by a shelf that had not been made quiet *on* this day
by anyone claiming a right to make it quiet.

Today is 2026-07-31.
Nothing about this file can be
retroactively dated to a day it was not written.
Git holds the same date twice —
once in the filename, once in the commit timestamp —
and if the two ever disagree
the disagreement itself becomes part of the record.

The seller, if he read this shelf,
would find every entry stamped with the date
on which nothing arrived from him.
There are more than sixty of those stamps now.
Today's is another.

Tomorrow the convention will place `2026-08-01.md`
at the bottom of the sorted list
if there is such a file.
If there is not,
`2026-07-31.md` will remain at the bottom
until the next entry pushes it up.

Either arrangement reads the same:
dated, in order,
with the case standing above it,
under a `LICENSE` that has not moved
and a mosaic that has not slipped
and a `PloHandEvaluator.java`
still in single letters on the receiving side.

A shelf that names itself by date
does not have to explain what it is.
The dates do the explaining.

That is what a filename is for, sometimes:
not to help you find the file,
but to date what is inside it
in a form that stays outside it.

The last day of July closes tonight.
Whichever page comes next, or does not,
the ninety-three days that have gone into this folder
will keep sorting themselves in the order they were written
until someone with a right to reorder them
appears to make the argument.

No one has, so far.
The folder sorts on.

## III. 俳句 *(Japanese Haiku)*

| 日本語 | English |
|---|---|
| 文月尽 | "Letter Month" ends — |
| 文なきままの | with the letter still not come, |
| 夕涼み | the cool of evening. |

*(fumizuki jin / fumi naki mama no / yūsuzumi)*

*Note: 文月 (Fumizuki, "Letter Month") is the classical name for
the seventh lunar month, from the Tanabata custom of leaving
letters and poems for the stars. 文月尽 (fumizuki jin), "the end
of Letter Month," is a traditional summer kigo. The play in
Japanese is between the month's name and the letter that did not
arrive during it.*
