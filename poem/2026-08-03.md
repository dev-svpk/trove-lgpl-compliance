# Poems for 2026-08-03

Three poems. Earlier days circled the restoration, the promise kept
by someone else, the readers before and after, the document that
endured, the equivalence at the depth, the two folders set side by
side, what the obfuscator could not reach in structure, the receipt
the seller would not issue, the collapsing arithmetic of repair,
the mosaic kept over his face, the polite prompt that opened the
lock, the *preferred form for modification*, the search yet to
happen, the other buyers, the key kept in the same room, what sat
next to it on the build path, the author we never met, what the
program had to say aloud, the foreground emptying as the seller
receded, the gate that earned the renames by being willing to
refuse them, the solver that priced the unknown but misread a
fixed rule, the smooth surface of the running product, the engine
that did not stop at the boundary the seller had drawn, the grant
already pressed into the package, the constant pool's small
numbered table at the side of each class, the folder the JVM would
not let him rename, the permission slip he typed in his own hand,
the ten-hour wall time that aged his refusal faster than he could
issue it, the PNG that kept what the chat would have let drift,
the decompiler's hand that signed each blank line with the next
letter of the alphabet, the recipe left on the shelf, the deed
filed beside the body, the side door at *for now*, the Coda's two
small songs in two languages, the shelf that keeps writing in
those same two forms one morning at a time, the two silences run
in parallel, the month the shelf went quiet, and the one file that
did not come back. Today's poems step back before all of that,
to the moment the case actually began — not the refusal, not the
pipeline, not the publish. Before any of that: a Google Drive
link opened in a browser, a folder listing scrolled through, and
a growing certainty that not one recognizable name was going to
appear. Nine hundred `.java` files under `gnu/trove/`, each one
labelled with the alphabet in single letters — `a.java`, `b.java`,
`c.java`, on and on. Whatever the seller thought he was
delivering, the delivery itself was the demonstration. The
license question was decided by the file listing before any
sentence was ever typed in a chat about it. The buyer's scroll
through that listing is the moment upstream of the whole
apparatus — the pipeline, the equivalence checks, the shelf, the
side door, the daily bow. All of it descends from what did not
appear in that folder on the first morning it was opened.

## I. The First Opening *(English)*

Before the pipeline —
before the token-stream check,
the bytecode round-trip,
the rename by upstream reference —
before any of it:

a link in a chat.
A folder in a cloud drive.
The click that opened it.

Inside: what he called *full source code.*
A JAR file. A build classpath.
And nine hundred files under `gnu/trove/`
whose names were the alphabet
in single letters —
`a.java`, `b.java`, `c.java`,
and on, past the fiftieth,
past the two hundredth,
past any threshold at which one recognizable name
might reasonably be expected to appear.

Not `TIntHashSet`.
Not `THashMap`.
Not `TLongObjectHashMap`.
Not one.

That was the moment the case began.

Not the refusal in the chat.
Not the doubling down two messages later.
Not the ten-hour pipeline.
Not the folder set beside the folder.
Not the deed filed at the head of the tree.

The case began in the file listing itself,
before a word was typed about it,
in the space between one filename and the next
where a name should have been.

The seller labelled the delivery
*full source code.*
The delivery answered the label
by containing none.

There was no accusation to make yet.
The folder had already made it,
alphabetically,
in the order the buyer scrolled.

Everything after — the polite request,
the refusal, the pipeline, the shelf,
the two-language coda, the small vigil in verse —
descends from a single scroll
through a directory listing
that produced no upstream name.

The case did not need to be argued.
It had already been demonstrated
by the file tree the seller sent,
in his own hand,
before he ever said a word about it.
