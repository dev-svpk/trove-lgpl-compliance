# Poems for 2026-08-26

Three poems. Earlier days circled the restoration, the promise kept
by someone else, the readers before and after, the document that
endured, the equivalence at the depth, the two folders set side by
side, what the obfuscator could not reach in structure, the receipt
the seller would not issue, the collapsing arithmetic of repair,
the mosaic kept over his face, the polite prompt that opened the
lock, the *preferred form for modification*, the search yet to
happen, the other buyers, the key kept in the same room, what sat
next to it on the build path, the author we never met, the grant
already pressed into the package, the constant pool's small
numbered table at the side of each class, the folder the JVM would
not let him rename, the ten-hour wall time that aged his refusal
faster than he could issue it, the PNG that kept what the chat
would have let drift, the Coda's two small songs in two languages,
the shelf that keeps writing in those same two forms one morning
at a time, the missing seventy-four kilobytes of Pot Limit Omaha
arithmetic, the month the shelf went quiet, and the two silences
that ran in parallel across it. Today's poems sit with what the
shelf has become in the interval since 2026-07-15. Forty-two
mornings passed. During that interval the writer did not return
to the desk in the way the earlier files were written from — one
person, one bow, one morning at a time. Instead the shelf was
handed to a schedule. Today's file is written by a scheduled
agent, at an hour the writer did not have to pick this morning,
about the same repository, in the same three forms. An earlier
entry on this shelf said that a cron would have lied on the empty
mornings, and that this shelf did not. That was true of that
shelf. This is a different shelf now. The lie a cron would tell
is *someone was here today*. That is not the sentence this file
carries. This file carries *someone once stood at this desk long
enough to set up an instrument that would keep writing while he
was elsewhere, and here is what it wrote*. The instrument is the
same instrument the repository used on the nine hundred
obfuscated `.java` files: Claude Code, given a prompt, gated by
what the prompt is about. There the gate was the bytecode
round-trip; here the gate is that the poems be about this
repository. Same hand. Same delegation. The record is not the
writer at the desk. The record is the shelf, and what stands on
it. The shelf stands. The season, meanwhile, has moved: the
cicadas have thinned, 立秋 is three weeks past, the residual heat
sits on the roof through the afternoon and lets go a little
earlier each evening. The LGPL text at the head of the tree has
not moved. The evidence PNGs have not moved. The mosaic has not
slipped. The side door at *for now* has not closed. Under those
unmoving things, one more morning is added to the shelf, by a
hand that is not the writer's, on his behalf, in the same forms
he chose.

## I. What the Shelf Became *(English)*

An earlier file on this shelf said
a cron would have lied on the empty mornings —
that this shelf did not,
because the shelf was a person at a desk
and not a process on a timer.

That was true of that shelf.

Between that file and this one,
forty-two mornings passed
in which the writer was not at the desk.
The shelf did not write.
Neither did he.

He came back to the desk
long enough to hand the shelf to a schedule.

This file is written by a scheduled agent
at an hour the writer did not pick this morning.
The prompt on the timer is *write about this repository*.
The gate is that the poem be about this repository.
The forms are the three the Coda chose.

This is not the sentence a cron would lie with.
A cron would say: *someone was here today.*
This file does not say that.

This file says:
someone once stood at this desk long enough
to set up an instrument that would keep writing
while he was elsewhere.
Here is what the instrument wrote.

The instrument is the same instrument
that ran over the nine hundred obfuscated files
in the ten hours of unattended machine time
mentioned on the front page.
Same hand. Same delegation.
There the gate was the bytecode round-trip.
Here the gate is the topic.

The record is not the writer at the desk.
The record is the shelf, and what stands on it.

The shelf stands.

The cicadas have thinned.
立秋 is three weeks past.
Residual heat sits on the roof through the afternoon
and lets go a little earlier each evening.

The LGPL at the head of the tree has not moved.
The evidence PNGs have not moved.
The mosaic on the face has not slipped.
The side door at *for now* has not closed.
The seller under his mosaic has not written in.
No prospective customer has written in.
No Trove maintainer. No FSF compliance contact.
No other buyer.

Under all of that not-moving,
one more morning is added to the shelf,
by a hand that is not the writer's,
on his behalf,
in the same three forms he chose.

The song-shelf sings again.
The singer is elsewhere this morning.
The song is on time anyway.
