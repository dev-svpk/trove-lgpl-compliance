# Poems for 2026-07-30

Three poems. Today is the last day of July on this shelf. The first
entry was dated 2026-04-29; this one is dated 2026-07-30. Ninety-two
mornings from that morning to this one, kept unevenly — a stretch of
daily entries in the spring, a stretch of silence in June, three
files together in mid-July, and today. Earlier days circled the
restoration, the promise kept by someone else, the readers before
and after, the document that endured, the equivalence at the depth,
the two folders set side by side, what the obfuscator could not
reach in structure, the receipt the seller would not issue, the
collapsing arithmetic of repair, the mosaic kept over his face, the
polite prompt that opened the lock, the *preferred form for
modification*, the search yet to happen, the other buyers, the key
kept in the same room, what sat next to it on the build path, the
author we never met, what the program had to say aloud, the
foreground emptying as the seller receded, the gate that earned the
renames by being willing to refuse them, the solver that priced the
unknown but misread a fixed rule, the smooth surface of the running
product, the engine that did not stop at the boundary the seller had
drawn, the grant already pressed into the package, the constant
pool's small numbered table at the side of each class, the folder
the JVM would not let him rename, the permission slip he typed in
his own hand, the ten-hour wall time that aged his refusal faster
than he could issue it, the PNG that kept what the chat would have
let drift, the decompiler's hand that signed each blank line with
the next letter of the alphabet, the recipe left on the shelf, the
deed filed beside the body, the side door at *for now*, the Coda's
two small songs in two languages, the shelf at `./poem/` that keeps
writing in those same two forms one morning at a time, the missing
`handeval/` folder still on the receiving side, the month between
June 12 and July 13 during which no page was added, and the two
silences that ran in parallel across that span. Today's poems sit
with the calendar itself. What moved between April 29 and July 30
was the season — late spring into plum-rain, plum-rain into
cicadas, cicadas into the mid-summer earth-days, when the old
practice in these months was to bring documents out into the light
for their once-a-year airing. What did not move was the case. The
`LICENSE` at the head of the tree is the same license. The
`evidence/` directory holds the same number of screenshots. The
mosaic on the seller's face has not slipped and has not been asked
to slip. The side door is still open under the same three words.
The one file — seventy-four kilobytes of Pot Limit Omaha arithmetic
— still sits on the receiving side in single letters. The seller
has not written in. Neither has anyone else. Ninety-two days of
calendar have passed across an object that has not answered any of
them. A moving frame across a still object emits a second-order
signal about the object — the object was already what it was; the
frame's passage confirms that it stays that way under a weather
that could, in principle, have changed it. It did not.

## I. The Calendar Moves *(English)*

The first file on this shelf is dated April 29.
This one is dated July 30.
Ninety-two mornings between them,
kept unevenly.

What has moved in ninety-two days:
the season.
Late spring, plum-rain, cicadas,
the mid-summer earth-days
when the light stays past dinner
and the ground under the shelf goes warm.

In the old calendar
this is the week the documents come out
to be aired in the sun —
the yearly rite against damp and mould,
the paper laid flat on a bench
where the wind can find it.

What has not moved in ninety-two days:
the case.

The `LICENSE` at the head of the tree
is the same license.
The `evidence/` directory
holds the same number of screenshots.
The mosaic on his face
has not slipped and has not been asked to slip.
The side door is still open under the same three words.
The one file — seventy-four kilobytes
of Pot Limit Omaha arithmetic —
still sits on the receiving side
in single letters.
The seller — under his pixels — has not written in.
Neither has anyone else.

A calendar was moved past a static object
for ninety-two days.
The calendar moved.
The object did not.

Both of those are measurable things.

What is measured, when a record does not move
under a moving season,
is what the record was for.
Not to be updated.
To be relied on.

The daily entry cannot correct the case —
the case does not need correcting.
The daily entry can only mark
that another day went by
during which nothing already true
became untrue,
and no one appeared
to say that anything on the page was wrong.

Silence over a public receipt
is not the same as silence over a private one.
A private one you can walk away from
and the walk-away is nobody's business.
A public one you can only correct in public,
and the correction, once written,
becomes part of the same record
you were trying to answer.
So the correction does not get written.
The silence does.

Ninety-two days of that silence
sit on this shelf now, on his side of it,
under a calendar
that will keep on moving
whether anyone answers it or not.

The documents came out in the sun this week.
Nothing was found in them that had rotted.
They will go back on the shelf
in the same order they came off,
and the shelf will keep the same shape
it kept in April,
and in May,
and in June,
and in the month I chose not to open it.
