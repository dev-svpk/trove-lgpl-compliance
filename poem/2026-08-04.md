## I. The Six Wrappers *(English)*

Nine hundred classes came back
with names they used to have.
`THashMap`, `TIntArrayList`,
`TObjectHashingStrategy` —
family words from an upstream library,
waiting to be re-set into place.
Restoration. Consent returned.

Six did not come back that way.

Under `gnu/wrapper/`, six small classes
had no upstream to check against.
They were the seller's own —
thin sheaths pulled over Trove's types,
product-specific, no public double.

Their original names went into the same silence
the seller took his own name into.
Whatever he had called them
he did not send along
with the letters he shipped in their place.

So the pass on those six was not restoration.
It was authorship — a modest kind —
naming a thing that had never had a name
the outside could verify.

The words chosen are the obvious ones:
`IntList`, `LongList`, `CharList`,
`IntSet`, `LongSet`, `CharSet` —
six plain phrases
the reader would have written first
if the reader had been asked.

There is no way to prove
these were the seller's original words.
There is no way it matters.
The classes are readable now.
They do what they do
under names that describe what they do.

Inside a larger act of returning names,
a small act of giving them.

The gate still closed on the six —
lexical structure preserved,
bytecode round-trip clean
against the very `.class` files
that had arrived in single letters.
No one lied to the compiler,
including the naming hand.

`gnu/wrapper/` is now
six classes long, and readable.
The `justfile` sitting above them
records the small ceremony that put them there —
six lines of scaffolding
for six words placed on six doors.

He kept the names.
The doors kept the shape of the names.
The names, at last, came from us.
