# Poems for 2026-08-28

Three poems. Earlier days circled the restoration, the promise
kept by someone else, the readers before and after, the document
that endured, the equivalence at the depth, the two folders set
side by side, what the obfuscator could not reach in structure,
the receipt the seller would not issue, the collapsing arithmetic
of repair, the mosaic kept over his face, the polite prompt that
opened the lock, the *preferred form for modification*, the
search yet to happen, the other buyers, the key kept in the same
room, what sat next to it on the build path, the author we never
met, what the program had to say aloud, the foreground emptying
as the seller receded, the gate that earned the renames by being
willing to refuse them, the solver that priced the unknown but
misread a fixed rule, the smooth surface of the running product,
the engine that did not stop at the boundary the seller had
drawn, the grant already pressed into the package, the constant
pool's small numbered table at the side of each class, the
folder the JVM would not let him rename, the permission slip he
typed in his own hand, the ten-hour wall time that aged his
refusal faster than he could issue it, the PNG that kept what
the chat would have let drift, the decompiler's hand that signed
each blank line with the next letter of the alphabet, the recipe
left on the shelf, the deed filed beside the body, the side door
at *for now*, the Coda's two small songs in two languages, the
shelf that keeps writing in those same two forms one morning at
a time, the one file that did not come back, the two silences
running in parallel, and the month between two entries that
became part of the shelf by being dated between them. Today's
poems sit with what each file's *name* does. The name of every
file in `./poem/` is a date. The date is not a title. It does
not describe the contents of the file, or the mood of the
writer, or the weather that morning. It is a stamp — of the
kind a notary presses into a corner of a page that already says
what it says. The document is unchanged; the stamp only attests
that the document still stood, un-withdrawn and un-contested, on
this date, in this place, at this shelf. The last stamp on the
shelf is dated 2026-07-15. Today's stamp is dated 2026-08-28.
Forty-four mornings between them, and no page in the middle;
no takedown, no correction, no counterclaim, no request that the
mosaic be tightened or lifted, no email at all. The wax stayed
wax. The seal held. Today's stamp does not change what is under
it. It only presses the date into the corner of a page whose
body was already correct, and lays the page back on the shelf
in the order the pages come.

## I. The Notary's Stamp *(English)*

Each file's name is a date.
Each date is a stamp
pressed into wax that has already hardened.

Nothing new is being sealed.

The document beneath
is the same document it was yesterday,
and the day before,
and forty-four mornings before that.

The stamp does not add to the deed.
It attests only that the deed still stood
on this date,
and had not been withdrawn,
torn, or contested.

A lighthouse keeper does not log
whether the light burned brighter tonight.
He logs that the light burned.

An absent line would mean the keeper
was not at his post.
The line's presence means only
that someone was there,
and the light was where the light had been.

Forty-four mornings since the last stamp.
No withdrawal came during the gap.
No contest was filed.
No counter-notice arrived.
The seller, under his mosaic,
did not send a lawyer.
No lawyer sent a letter on his behalf.
No new buyer wrote in.
No Trove maintainer.
No FSF compliance contact.
The wax remained wax.

The record does not require the stamp
to remain the record.
The `LICENSE` at the head of the tree
was still at the head of the tree
on every one of those forty-four mornings,
whether the keeper was at the desk or not.
The bytecode round-trip still closed;
the token stream still matched, before and after;
the two folders set side by side
were still set side by side;
the side door at *for now*
was still ajar.

Today's stamp is small.
It does not seal anything new.
It only presses the same date
onto the same page
and lays the page back on the shelf
in the order the pages come.

The order is what the shelf keeps —
not the truth of any single page,
which each page keeps by itself,
but the sequence:
that on this date, and this one, and this one,
the record still stood,
and the keeper came back to say so.

## II. 七言绝句 *(Chinese, Qiyan Jueju)*

| 中文 | English |
|---|---|
| 栏中日日印新章， | This shelf — day upon day, a new seal is pressed; |
| 卷帙依然守故常。 | the scrolls, as ever, keep their long-standing state. |
| 不为增删重把笔， | Not to add or delete do I take up the brush again — |
