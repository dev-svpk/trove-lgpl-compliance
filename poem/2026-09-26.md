# Poems for 2026-09-26

Three poems. The file tree does not change with seasons; outside
it, the harvest moon is on the way. Today's English poem sits
with the small formulaic phrase that closes the output of every
bytecode-equivalence check in this repository — *modulo
identifier names and the constant pool* — and the case that
lives on the residue side of that clause. The Chinese quatrain
and the Japanese haiku take autumn.

---

## I. Modulo Names *(English)*

The check says: *same, modulo identifier names
and the constant pool.*
Two English words carrying a Latin hinge.

*Modulo* is the machinist's shrug —
setting aside a quantity as immaterial
to the thing being counted.
Bring `a`. Bring `THashMap`.
The bytecode folds them into the same offsets.
The JVM, running,
reads either as the same call.

On the running side of the boundary
the shrug is fair.
The name is not what `main` needs.

But *modulo* is not what the license said.
The license said: *preferred form for modification.*
What a reader needs
to open the code and change what it does.

On the reading side,
`a` is a wall
and `THashMap` is a hall
with a bench along one side of it
you can sit down on
and see what happens next.

Two things that fold to the same bytecode
under one arithmetic
are two different things
under another.

The seller counted the first way.
He shipped the low-residue copy
and called the residue nothing —
called it *source.*

The check we wrote counts the first way, too,
because that is the only fair way to prove
we did not add or subtract behavior.
The whole point of running it, though,
was to show that a different arithmetic
had been quietly at work in the shipping —
one whose remainder was the entire point.

*Modulo names* is where the case lives.
Everything on the identity side of that clause
is the machine's business.
Everything on the residue side
belongs to the reader.

We put the residue back
one file at a time.

## II. 七言绝句 *(Chinese, Qiyan Jueju)*

| 中文 | English |
|---|---|
| 秋分陌上稻低头， | At autumn equinox, along the field-paths, the rice bows its head; |
| 千字归田各识丘。 | a thousand characters return to their fields — each knows its own mound. |
| 售者旧封犹掩迹， | The seller's old envelope still conceals its trace; |
| 名归此卷更何求。 | but names have returned to this volume — what more is there to seek? |
