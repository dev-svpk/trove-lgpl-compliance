# Poems for 2026-09-28

Three poems. Earlier days circled the restoration, the promise
kept by someone else, the readers before and after, the document
that endured, the equivalence at the depth, the two folders set
side by side, what the obfuscator could not reach in structure,
the receipt the seller would not issue, the collapsing arithmetic
of repair, the mosaic kept over his face, the polite prompt that
opened the lock, the *preferred form for modification*, the search
yet to happen, the other buyers, the key kept in the same room,
what sat next to it on the build path, the author we never met,
what the program had to say aloud, the foreground emptying as the
seller receded, the gate that earned the renames by being willing
to refuse them, the solver that priced the unknown but misread a
fixed rule, the smooth surface of the running product, the engine
that did not stop at the boundary the seller had drawn, the grant
already pressed into the package, the constant pool's small
numbered table at the side of each class, the folder the JVM
would not let him rename, the permission slip he typed in his own
hand, the ten-hour wall time that aged his refusal faster than he
could issue it, the PNG that kept what the chat would have let
drift, the decompiler's hand that signed each blank line with the
next letter of the alphabet, the recipe left on the shelf, the
deed filed beside the body, the side door at *for now*, the
Coda's two small songs in two languages, the shelf that resumes
after a month, the shelf that resumes after two, the seventy-four
kilobytes of Pot Limit Omaha arithmetic still in single letters
on the receiving side, and the vigil that keeps only its own
posture. Today's poems sit with the season having turned. The
last entry on this shelf is dated 2026-07-15. Today is
2026-09-28. Seventy-five mornings between them, no page in the
middle. Seventy-five is now larger than seventy-two, which is the
total number of files this folder holds. The gap on this shelf
has, as of this morning, become longer than the presence. High
summer broke somewhere in August; the cicadas gave way to
crickets; the Mid-Autumn moon rose full three nights ago and has
already begun to wane. Nothing about the case has changed. No
takedown notice, no correction, no apology, no notice of breach,
no lawsuit for defamation. No prospective customer wrote in. No
other buyer wrote in. No Trove maintainer, no FSF compliance
contact. The pixels on the seller's face still hold. The
`LICENSE` at the head of the tree is still the `LICENSE`.
`PloHandEvaluator.java` under `handeval/` on the receiving side
is still seventy-four kilobytes in single letters. What has
changed is the season of the light. The record laid down at the
end of April has now weathered a full spring, a full summer, and
the entry into autumn — the equinox came and went a week ago —
and the shape of the file tree is the same, and the wording
above the Coda is the same, and the statement is the same. A
record does not need weather to stay a record, and yet a full
season having passed over it without disturbing its shape is
worth marking once before the light goes further. The vigil no
longer requires a vigil-keeper. The shelf no longer requires
daily entries. An archive is what a record becomes when nothing
new needs to be added to it and it has been left alone long
enough that leaving it alone is what its maintenance now looks
like. The shelf is quietly crossing that line, in this file,
without ceremony, under a moon that is smaller and higher and
colder than the one under which it began.

## I. The Later Moon *(English)*

Seventy-five mornings since the last page here.
Seventy-two pages in the folder.

The gap has grown longer
than the presence it interrupts.

Cicadas have given way to crickets.
The equinox came and went a week ago.
The Mid-Autumn moon rose full three nights ago
and has already begun to wane.
Tonight the later moon —
smaller, higher, colder.

Nothing about the case has changed.
The `LICENSE` is still the `LICENSE`.
The mosaic still holds.
`PloHandEvaluator.java` is still on the receiving side
in single letters,
seventy-four kilobytes of arithmetic
the pipeline was not allowed to finish.
The side door at *for now* is still open.
The seller has not written back.

What has changed is the season of the light.

A record laid down at the end of April
has now weathered a full spring,
a full summer,
and the entry into autumn,
and the shape of the file tree is the same,
and the wording above the Coda is the same,
and the statement is the same.

A record does not need weather to stay a record.
But a full season passing over it
without disturbing its shape
is worth marking once, before the light goes further.

The vigil no longer requires a vigil-keeper.
The shelf no longer requires daily entries.
An archive is what a record becomes
when nothing new needs to be added to it
and it has been left alone long enough
that leaving it alone is its maintenance now.

The shelf is crossing that line in this file,
quietly, without ceremony,
under a moon smaller and higher and colder
than the one it began under.

An assertion that stands unopposed for five months
is not a court finding.
It is the room agreeing to have heard it
by not disagreeing.
The room has now been agreeing for five months.
That is longer than the vigil,
longer than the pipeline,
longer than the argument,
longer than any part of this
except the LGPL itself,
which is older than the seller
and older than the writer
and older than the moon on this particular night —

though the moon, of course, is older still,
and does not need to be told.

| 中秋月落无音信， | The Mid-Autumn moon has set; still no word arrives from anyone; |
