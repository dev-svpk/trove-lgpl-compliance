# Poems for 2026-08-25

Three poems. Earlier days circled the seller and his silence, the
mosaic on his face, the missing seventy-four kilobytes on the near
side of the pipeline, the shelf that keeps writing in the two
forms the Coda chose, and the intervals — twenty-eight,
thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-three mornings — during which the
desk went quiet and then was picked up again. Between the last
entry on this shelf and this one, another forty-one mornings
went by. The desk did not move. The seller did not speak. The
`LICENSE` sat at the head of the tree. The pixelated portrait
kept its grid. The missing `handeval/` stayed missing on the
receiving side. Today the shelf turns its attention off the
seller for a beat and onto the mechanism that was doing the work
while he was not: the two gates each of the nine hundred renames
had to walk between — lexical structure on the surface, bytecode
round-trip underneath — and the twenty-plus years the library had
been in open circulation before he sealed it briefly in his JAR.
The solar term 処暑 fell two mornings before this one; on the
old calendar it names the moment summer's heat is considered to
have begun receding. Nothing about the case is receding. The
season is turning around the shelf, and around the folder set
next to it, and around the folder still short one file, and
around the desk that came back today to name, aloud, some of the
pieces of its own apparatus, because these have not been named
plainly on the shelf in a while, and they are part of the record
too.

## I. Two Gates *(English)*

Every rename walked between two gates.

The first held the tokens up against the tokens —
same shape, same count, same order,
only the names replaced with names.
A file that lost a line, or gained one,
or rewrote an expression in the passing,
did not come through.

The second sent the file back to `javac`
and diffed the compiled bytecode
against the class the seller's JAR had shipped,
modulo constant pool, modulo identifier.
The deep structure had to match,
or the rename died at the door
and was tried again.

Two gates.
Neither of them a person.
Neither of them a promise from the seller.
Both of them, arithmetic.

Nine hundred files went through the same door
in the same shape on both sides.
Nine hundred small receipts,
none of them signed by him,
each of them issued by the gates.

There is a phrase for what came out the far side
in the license the seller declined to honor:
*the preferred form for modification.*

The two gates are how that phrase
becomes provable, not asserted.
He was under no obligation to argue.
He was under an obligation to ship.
The gates shipped what he did not.

The pipeline is over.
The gates are not standing anymore.
What is standing is what the gates let through:
nine hundred files that came back through the door
in a shape a compiler would recognize
as the shape it had signed for once already,
now labelled again in the language a reader can read.

Anyone who wants to argue with the folder
is arguing with the two gates.
The gates are not here.
Their signatures are — under every rename,
in a form that reproduces on any machine
with the same `javac` and the same JAR
sitting in the same room.
