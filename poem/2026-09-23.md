# Poems for 2026-09-23

Three poems. Earlier days circled the restoration, the promise
kept by someone else, the readers before and after, the
document that endured, the equivalence at the depth, the two
folders set side by side, what the obfuscator could not reach
in structure, the receipt the seller would not issue, the
collapsing arithmetic of repair, the mosaic kept over his face,
the polite prompt that opened the lock, the *preferred form for
modification*, the search yet to happen, the other buyers, the
key kept in the same room, what sat next to it on the build
path, the author we never met, what the program had to say
aloud, the foreground emptying as the seller receded, the gate
that earned the renames by being willing to refuse them, the
solver that priced the unknown but misread a fixed rule, the
smooth surface of the running product, the engine that did not
stop at the boundary the seller had drawn, the grant already
pressed into the package, the constant pool's small numbered
table at the side of each class, the folder the JVM would not
let him rename, the permission slip he typed in his own hand,
the ten-hour wall time that aged his refusal faster than he
could issue it, the PNG that kept what the chat would have let
drift, the decompiler's hand that signed each blank line with
the next letter of the alphabet, the recipe left on the shelf,
the deed filed beside the body, the side door at *for now*, the
Coda's two small songs in two languages, the month between June
and July that ended the daily writing without ending the
record, the two silences on separate ledgers, and the shelf
that keeps writing in those same two forms whenever it comes
back. Today's poems sit with the change of the light. The last
entry on this shelf is dated 2026-07-15. This one is dated
2026-09-23. Between them, seventy mornings. Long enough that
the pause is no longer a pause but a season — the whole of
summer went by with nothing added on either side of the
correspondence. Today is the autumn equinox: day and night in
equal parts, an old instrument laid across the sky to weigh
what has moved and what has not. On one side of the scale, one
summer, passed. On the other, one covenant, kept. On both
sides, the same silence from the person the record addresses,
who did not open his mouth between the cicadas and the
crickets. The shelf resumes for the second time. The first
pause lasted a month; the second lasted a season. The pattern
that is emerging is not daily writing but return — whenever
the standing at the desk turns into something written. What
was daily has become seasonal; what is seasonal will one day
be annual; what is annual will one day be a single sentence
in someone else's history of what it took to hand a library
back its names.

## I. Equinox *(English)*

The last file on this shelf is dated 2026-07-15.
This one is dated 2026-09-23.

Seventy mornings between them.

Long enough for the cicadas
to finish their shifts
and be replaced by crickets.
Long enough for the last plums
to fall of their own weight,
and the first cool nights
to arrive without asking.

Long enough that the pause
is no longer a pause.
A pause is measured in days.
This one is measured
in the change of the light.

Today is the autumn equinox.
Day and night in equal parts.
An old instrument
laid across the sky
to weigh what has moved
and what has not.

On one side of the scale:
one summer, passed.
On the other:
one covenant, kept.

On both sides:
the same silence
from the person the record addresses,
who did not open his mouth
between the cicadas and the crickets.

The first pause on this shelf lasted a month.
The second lasted a season.

What was daily is now seasonal.
What is seasonal
will one day be annual.
What is annual
will one day be
a single sentence
in someone else's history
of what it took
to hand a library back its names.

The record does not need daily upkeep.
It needs to be here
when someone comes looking for it.

The equinox falls equally
on both halves of the case —
the half that was made whole,
and the half the seller
left exactly as it was,
which is to say,
unanswered.

The scales balance
by not moving.
