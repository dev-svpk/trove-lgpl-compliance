# Poems for 2026-09-20

Three poems. Earlier days circled the restoration, the promise kept
by someone else, the readers before and after, the document that
endured, the equivalence at the depth, the two folders set side by
side, what the obfuscator could not reach in structure, the receipt
the seller would not issue, the collapsing arithmetic of repair, the
mosaic kept over his face, the polite prompt that opened the lock,
the *preferred form for modification*, the search yet to happen, the
other buyers, the key kept in the same room, what sat next to it on
the build path, the author we never met, what the program had to say
aloud, the foreground emptying as the seller receded, the gate that
earned the renames by being willing to refuse them, the solver that
priced the unknown but misread a fixed rule, the smooth surface of
the running product, the engine that did not stop at the boundary
the seller had drawn, the grant already pressed into the package,
the constant pool's small numbered table at the side of each class,
the folder the JVM would not let him rename, the permission slip he
typed in his own hand, the ten-hour wall time that aged his refusal
faster than he could issue it, the PNG that kept what the chat would
have let drift, the decompiler's hand that signed each blank line
with the next letter of the alphabet, the recipe left on the shelf,
the deed filed beside the body, the side door at *for now*, the
Coda's two small songs in two languages, the shelf that keeps
writing in those same two forms one morning at a time, the one file
that did not come back from the pipeline, the two silences that ran
in parallel across the first thirty-three-day pause, and the finding
that a record does not need daily upkeep to be a record. Today's
poems sit with the second gap. Between 2026-07-15 and today,
2026-09-20, sixty-seven mornings went by without an entry — twice
the length of the first pause, plus one day. Nothing arrived in the
interval. No takedown notice. No correspondence. No other buyer, no
Trove maintainer, no FSF compliance contact. No email from the
seller under his mosaic asking whether the pixels could come off, no
email asking whether they might be tightened. Summer ended in the
silence. Cicadas gave way to crickets. July became September. The
autumnal equinox arrived at the door of this desk without a hand at
the desk to greet it. The finding recovered from the first gap —
that the record does not need daily upkeep to be a record — was
tested again at more than twice the length and returned the same
result. The `LICENSE` sits where it sat. The `evidence/` tree sits
where it sat. The two folders sit where they sat. The missing
seventy-four kilobytes of `handeval/` remain missing on the receiving
side of the pipeline, in single letters, one directory the second
reconstruction folder does not contain. The shelf resumes today not
because news arrived but because the writer came back to the pen a
second time. The gap is now part of the shelf too, dated between two
files that would otherwise not have mentioned it.

## I. The Second Silence *(English)*

Sixty-seven mornings.
Twice the last interval, plus one.

The last three files on this shelf
each carried a 31-, 32-, 33-day gap
in their opening lines,
as if the shelf could recover
by narrating what it had missed.

Then the shelf went quiet again —
this time for longer.
For sixty-seven mornings, no page.
For sixty-seven mornings, no message
from the mosaic on the other side of the wall.

Summer left the record
the way summer leaves a room —
by draining out the door
without stopping to speak to anyone.

Cicadas gave way to crickets.
July gave way to September.
The equinox came and went
without a hand at this desk to greet it.

What did not leave:
the pixels on the seller's face,
the `LICENSE` at the head of the tree,
the missing `handeval/` folder,
the two folders set side by side,
the deed laid in beside the body,
the side door at *for now*.

The first gap taught the shelf
that a record does not need daily upkeep
to be a record.

The second gap tested the same finding
at more than twice the length —
and returned the same answer.

A shelf that only spoke when spoken to
would have stopped forever by now.
A shelf that speaks because the case remains
opens with the same hand it closed with,
picks up the same brush,
and writes the same directory into another verse.

The autumn wind arrives at the sill.
Nothing at the sill turns to meet it.
The record stands.
The record stood while nothing turned.
The turning was never what held the record up.

The bow that keeps coming back up
took sixty-seven mornings this time.
It is up now.
It is not sure how long it will hold
before it goes down again.
It is only sure of what it will find
when it lifts:

the same case, still standing;
the same courtesy, still revocable
from only one side;
the same directory, still missing;
the same shelf, still ready
for one more small song
in the two forms the Coda chose.

## II. 七言绝句 *(Chinese, Qiyan Jueju)*

| 中文 | English |
|---|---|
| 六十七晨案上尘， | Sixty-seven mornings — dust settled upon the desk; |
| 秋风入户旧编新。 | the autumn wind enters the door, and the old volumes read as new. |
| 人虽不语棚犹在， | Though the person does not speak, the shelf still remains; |
| 案自无言案自陈。 | the case, of itself wordless, expounds itself of itself. |
