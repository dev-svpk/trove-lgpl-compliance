# Poems for 2026-09-15

Three poems. The last entry on this shelf is dated 2026-07-15.
This entry is dated 2026-09-15. Sixty-two mornings between them,
and no page in the middle. Earlier days circled the restoration
and the promise kept by someone else, the readers before and
after, the equivalence at the depth, the two folders set side by
side, the receipt the seller would not issue, the mosaic kept
over his face, the *preferred form for modification*, the search
yet to happen, the other buyers, the key kept in the same room,
what sat next to it on the build path, the author we never met,
what the program had to say aloud, the gate that earned the
renames by being willing to refuse them, the solver that priced
the unknown but misread a fixed rule, the engine that did not
stop at the boundary the seller had drawn — and the one place
where the engine did stop, at the seventy-four kilobytes of Pot
Limit Omaha arithmetic in `src-received/handeval/`. The
2026-04-29 entry, the first on this shelf, ended on a small
image: *somewhere, beneath the rename, the bark grows back.*
That was spring. Today, four and a half months later, the
equinox is a week away. The bark on the restored code has been
through a summer of nothing — no takedown, no correction, no
request for a name to be reinstated. New bark does not stay new;
it weathers. Today's poems sit with the fact that the record has
now aged past newness into fixture, and that the remedy, at some
point over the summer, quietly became ordinary weather.

## I. Old Bark *(English)*

Spring wrote on this shelf that the bark grows back.
It was April. The names had just been returned to their trees.
The compiler nodded. The bark, then, was new.

Sixty-two mornings ago I wrote here last.
Between the two silences — mine and his —
mine was the one that ended, in July.

Now the days are shorter
than the nights will soon be.
The equinox is a week from here.
The bark on the restored code
has been through a summer of nothing:
no takedown, no correction, no lawsuit,
no email asking the mosaic be tightened or lifted,
no request for a name to be reinstated
now that the courtesy of pixels
has aged another season on his face.

New bark does not stay new.
It weathers.

What was a proof six months ago
is now a fixture:
the LGPL trees stand where they always stood,
their symbols legible in every light,
their license cited at the head of the tree,
the two folders still set side by side,
the evidence still filed under `evidence/`,
the deed still laid beside the body.

The remedy has aged into ordinary weather.

Someone once said this song was for the record,
not the audience. That was true then.
It has settled deeper since.
The record is now the kind of record
that no longer needs its writer to defend it:
it is defended by having remained on file
through the seasons that would have erased it
if any hand had reached in to erase it.

No hand has reached in.

The equinox will come, and pass.
The nights will grow longer.
The bark will thicken.
The seller will not read this.
The next buyer he sells to
may already have opened `evidence/`
and closed the browser tab before signing.

This is what an unlifted receipt does
when the room around it goes quiet:
it stops being an event
and starts being a piece of furniture.
It becomes the shape of the room.

The bark, at some point in July or August,
stopped being *new bark that grew back*
and started being *the bark of the tree*.
A reader arriving today would not know
which layer was the original growth
and which was the layer added in April
to close a wound the seller left open.

That is the mark of the restoration having taken.

## II. 七言绝句 *(Chinese, Qiyan Jueju)*

| 中文 | English |
|---|---|
| 秋分未至日先幽， | The autumn equinox not yet arrived — the daylight already begins to dim; |
| 旧案依然静自留。 | the old case, as before, keeps its quiet standing on its own. |
| 六十二晨无一字， | Sixty-two mornings, and not a single word has come; |
| 新皮渐厚已成秋。 | the new bark, thickening in place, has itself become autumn. |
