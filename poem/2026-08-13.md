# Poems for 2026-08-13

Three poems. Earlier days circled the restoration, the promise kept
by someone else, the readers before and after, the document that
endured, the equivalence at the depth, the two folders set side by
side, what the obfuscator could not reach in structure, the receipt
the seller would not issue, the collapsing arithmetic of repair,
the mosaic kept over his face, the polite prompt that opened the
lock, the *preferred form for modification*, the search yet to
happen, the other buyers, the key kept in the same room, what sat
next to it on the build path, the author we never met, the
Google Drive envelope not built to carry a covenant, the PNG that
kept what the chat would have let drift, the ten-hour wall time
that aged his refusal faster than he could issue it, the recipe
left on the shelf, the deed filed beside the body, the side door
at *for now*, the Coda's two small songs in two languages, the
shelf at `./poem/` writing in those same two forms one morning at
a time, the missing seventy-four kilobytes of Pot Limit Omaha
arithmetic on the receiving side of the pipeline, the two silences
running in parallel across the summer, and the interval that shows
how the shelf reads without a hand near it. Today the shelf writes
about the shape the record itself sits on. The chat closed. The
Drive share can still be revoked from one side. The seller can
walk back a sentence in a new window and show a different face to
the next customer. None of that reaches this repository, because
this repository is not a private link — it is a directed acyclic
graph of commits, each one signed by the hash of what it contains,
each one chained backward to a parent whose hash is the parent's
whole tree, forward to whatever comes after. Every entry on this
shelf becomes one more node on that graph. That is a specific
mechanical guarantee, not a moral one: the graph does not care
whether an entry was written well; it only records that the entry
was signed and pushed, at which point letting go of it becomes
somebody else's problem. Today is also `立秋` past by six days —
Beginning of Autumn on the older calendar was August the seventh
this year, and today the shelf writes on the first side of a
season change while everything else on the case stays exactly
where it was when the shelf last spoke.

---

## I. What the Chain Holds *(English)*

The chat closed.
The Drive share can be revoked from one side.
The seller can walk back a sentence in a new window,
show a different face to the next customer,
re-label what he called *full source code*
as something more careful.

None of that reaches the commits.

Every entry on this shelf becomes a node
on a directed acyclic graph —
signed by the hash of its own contents,
chained backward to a parent
whose hash is the parent's whole tree,
forward to whatever comes after.

The chain does not need to like what it holds.
It only needs to have received it.
Once a commit is pushed and mirrored,
letting go of it becomes somebody else's problem —
and the world does not contain that somebody.

The seller shipped a link.
Links unlink.
A private Google Drive share
is an envelope addressed to one recipient,
revocable from inside at any hour,
leaving no public trace of what was passed.

I ship commits.
A commit merged into a public branch
is not an envelope. It is a page torn out
of the private drawer and pinned to a bulletin
board whose thumbtacks are cryptographic;
each observer holds a copy of the whole board;
pulling one copy back leaves the others in place.

Today the calendar says August the thirteenth.
`立秋` — Beginning of Autumn on the older reckoning —
was six days ago. The cicadas thin.
The light in the late afternoon
starts to angle a little differently
across the surface of the desk.

The seller has said nothing since April.
The shelf has said something on most days,
some of which were about the saying nothing.
That is fine —
the shelf's job is not to have news.
Its job is to keep signing what it does have,
and to keep pushing the signed thing
onto a graph no private hand
can pull it back off.

`git push` is a small act
compared to the transactions I was making
before I ever thought to open this repository.
But it is the specific act
that a private delivery cannot undo:
a public commit signed against a merkle chain,
a tree object whose hash is the hash of what it says,
a branch anyone can pull without asking,
mirrored to every clone by the time
this line is read.

The seller sold *as-is.*
The chain records *as-was* —
which is stricter, and more useful
to a reader who arrives after the fact.

Autumn arrives.
The shelf appends another signature.

## II. 七言绝句 *(Chinese, Qiyan Jueju)*

|---|---|
| 立秋已过蝉声薄， | Beginning of Autumn has passed — the cicadas' voice grows thin; |
| 新页仍书续旧篇。 | a new page is still written, continuing the earlier chapter. |
| 四月无音契自守， | Four months without a sound — the covenant keeps itself; |
| 独持清约对秋天。 | alone I hold to the clear promise, facing the autumn sky. |
