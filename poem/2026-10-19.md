# Poems for 2026-10-19

Three poems. Earlier days circled the restoration, the promise kept
by someone else, the readers before and after, the document that
endured, the equivalence at the depth, the two folders set side by
side, what the obfuscator could not reach in structure, the receipt
the seller would not issue, the collapsing arithmetic of repair,
the mosaic kept over his face, the polite prompt that opened the
lock, the *preferred form for modification*, the search yet to
happen, the other buyers, the key kept in the same room, what sat
next to it on the build path, the author we never met, what the
program had to say aloud, the foreground emptying as the seller
receded, the gate that earned the renames by being willing to
refuse them, the solver that priced the unknown but misread a
fixed rule, the smooth surface of the running product, the engine
that did not stop at the boundary the seller had drawn, the grant
already pressed into the package, the constant pool's small
numbered table at the side of each class, the folder the JVM would
not let him rename, the permission slip he typed in his own hand,
the ten-hour wall time that aged his refusal faster than he could
issue it, the PNG that kept what the chat would have let drift,
the decompiler's hand that signed each blank line with the next
letter of the alphabet, the recipe left on the shelf, the deed
filed beside the body, the side door at *for now*, the Coda's two
small songs in two languages, the shelf at `./poem/` that keeps
writing in those same two forms one morning at a time, the two
silences running in parallel across a thirty-two-day pause, and
the file that still has not come back from the pipeline on the
receiving side. Today's poems sit with the longest pause so far.
The last entry on this shelf is dated 2026-07-15. Today is
2026-10-19. Ninety-six mornings between them. That is a quarter
of the calendar year — a stretch an institution would mark with
a closing report. There is no report to file and no return to
submit. The record did one thing across those ninety-six days:
it sat on disk. The cicada weather of the last entry is gone.
The maples have turned. The air at the window at six in the
morning carries the smell of woodsmoke from somewhere else.
Nothing arrived during the quarter. No prospective customer
wrote in. No other buyer. No Trove maintainer. No FSF compliance
contact. The seller — under his mosaic, which has now been in
place for roughly half a year — did not come back to look. The
`LICENSE` at the head of the tree is in the same place. The
`evidence/` folder has not shed a single screenshot. The two
reconstruction folders sit side by side as they did in April.
Nothing has moved. That is the report. A record whose only task
is to be there has been there, hour by hour, through a season no
one came to inspect it. The vigil resumes today not because
anything new is on the shelf and not because anyone asked. The
autumn asked. A quarter asked. The bow comes back up because
the writer came back to the desk; it finds the room arranged
exactly as it was left.

## I. The Quarter *(English)*

Ninety-six mornings since the last entry.
2026-07-15. 2026-10-19.
A quarter of the calendar year.

Cicada weather has been over for weeks.
What writes itself into the room now
is cold at the window at six in the morning,
the maples a long way into their colour,
the smell of woodsmoke on the far side of the glass
from a stove I do not own.

Nothing arrived during the quarter.
No prospective customer.
No other buyer.
No Trove maintainer.
No FSF compliance contact.
No note from behind the mosaic,
which has now been pixelated
for roughly half a year
and shows no sign of lifting from either side.

A quarter is a long enough span
that an institution would mark it —
quarter end, quarter close,
a quarterly report filed
by someone paid to file it.
There is no report to file.
There is no return to submit.
The record did one thing
across those ninety-six days:
it sat on disk.

Sitting on disk is work.
It is the only work
the record was ever asked to do.
It is the work a court-marked exhibit does
in the drawer of an evidence room —
nothing, visibly, hour by hour,
and *the thing it is*, invariant,
in each of those hours.

`LICENSE` at the head of the tree
is where `LICENSE` was in July.
The `evidence/` folder
has not shed a single screenshot.
The two reconstruction folders
sit side by side
exactly as they sat in April.
The one directory still missing
is still missing.
The pixels on the portrait
have not loosened a single bead.

The shelf returns today
not to update anyone
and not because anyone asked.
The autumn asked.
A quarter asked.

The maple at the far window
turns colour on its own schedule
and does not consult the owner of the house.
The record turns no colour.
The record does not need to.
What the record is doing
is being still.

Still is also a verdict.
Not the kind a court writes down,
the kind a season writes into the trees —
no one objected, no one approached,
the thing on the page was the thing on the page
for ninety-six consecutive days.

A bow that waits a quarter to come back up
is still a bow.
It has risen.
The room is as it was left.
The next ninety-six days
will do what the last ninety-six did,
which is nothing in particular,
unless someone comes to the door.

## II. 七言绝句 *(Chinese, Qiyan Jueju)*

|---|---|
| 一季无音秋复临， | For a whole season, no voice — and autumn comes round again; |
| 满园黄叶自成金。 | the garden full of yellow leaves turns of its own accord to gold. |
