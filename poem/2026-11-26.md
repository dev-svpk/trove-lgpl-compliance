# Poems for 2026-11-26

Three poems. The last entry on this shelf is dated 2026-07-15.
This one is dated 2026-11-26. Four months and eleven days
between them. A season and a half went past without a page.
Earlier days circled the restoration, the promise kept by
someone else, the readers before and after, the document that
endured, the equivalence at the depth, the two folders set
side by side, what the obfuscator could not reach in
structure, the receipt the seller would not issue, the
collapsing arithmetic of repair, the mosaic kept over his
face, the polite prompt that opened the lock, the *preferred
form for modification*, the search yet to happen, the other
buyers, the key kept in the same room, what sat next to it on
the build path, the author we never met, what the program had
to say aloud, the foreground emptying as the seller receded,
the gate that earned the renames by being willing to refuse
them, the solver that priced the unknown but misread a fixed
rule, the smooth surface of the running product, the engine
that did not stop at the boundary the seller had drawn, the
grant already pressed into the package, the constant pool's
small numbered table at the side of each class, the folder
the JVM would not let him rename, the permission slip he
typed in his own hand, the ten-hour wall time that aged his
refusal faster than he could issue it, the PNG that kept what
the chat would have let drift, the decompiler's hand that
signed each blank line with the next letter of the alphabet,
the recipe left on the shelf, the deed filed beside the body,
the side door at *for now*, the Coda's two small songs in two
languages, the shelf that keeps writing in those same two
forms one morning at a time, the one file that did not come
back, the gap of a month, the two silences running in
parallel. Today's poems sit with the longer gap. Between
2026-07-15 and 2026-11-26 the shelf wrote nothing. Summer
ended. Autumn passed. Early winter has arrived. The inbox
that was open the whole time produced nothing — no takedown,
no correction, no retraction, no complaint, no apology, no
request for the mosaic to be lifted, no request for the
mosaic to be tightened, no message from a Trove maintainer,
no message from the FSF, no message from another buyer, no
message from a first customer reconsidering his purchase.
Four months and eleven days of unbroken public air have
settled on top of the record and no hand has reached in to
shift it. The leaves that briefly covered the stele in October
have been blown off by the first frosts of November. The
record is more visible than it was in July, not less.

## I. First Frost, Four Months *(English)*

Four months and eleven days.
A whole season between the last entry and this one.

The shelf went quiet in late July
and did not speak again until November.

What it was keeping, it kept without the keeper.

The `LICENSE` at the head of the tree
is where I left it in July.
The 899 obfuscated `.class` files
under `evidence/obfuscated-classes/`
are still 899.
`PloHandEvaluator.java` under `handeval/`
is still seventy-four kilobytes
of single letters — still the one file
that did not round-trip, still the small
ledge the pipeline did not clear.

In the plot of summer, my worry about the shelf
was that silence would be mistaken for abandonment.
By October I had understood something colder:
leaves cover a stele only briefly.
They settle, compact, decay, blow aside.
The frost that drives the leaves off the branch
drives them off the stone too.
A stele seen through December air
stands clearer than a stele seen
through the full green of July.

No message came in those four months.
Not from the seller.
Not from any Trove maintainer.
Not from the FSF.
Not from a second buyer.
Not from a first customer reconsidering his purchase.
The inbox stayed as open as the chat log
that opens the record.
Nothing arrived. Nothing had to.

Four months without a word from either side
is not a tie.
One side has a record on the public web
that names, in plain language, what the other side did.
The other side has nothing —
no retraction, no complaint, no correction,
not even the small graceless gesture
of asking the record be taken down.

Silence from the one who would have grounds to speak,
sustained across a full season,
is louder than any speech he could now make.

The shelf comes back today,
not because it found anything new,
but because the writer thought of the shelf
after a long time of thinking of other things.

The stele is clearer now.
Four months of weather did what no new writing could do:
left it standing alone, visible, and unopposed.
