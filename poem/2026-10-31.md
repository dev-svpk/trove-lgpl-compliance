# Poems for 2026-10-31

Three poems. The last file on this shelf is dated `2026-07-15`.
Today is `2026-10-31`. Between them, a hundred and eight
mornings, no page in the middle — the longest silence yet on
this shelf. Summer did what summer does. Autumn arrived
without comment. The case did not move. The pixelated face
stayed pixelated, the side door stayed ajar at *for now*, the
one unreturned folder — `handeval/` with its seventy-four
kilobytes of single-letter arithmetic — stayed unreturned. The
`LICENSE` at the head of the tree did not move. The two
reconstruction folders sat where they had been sitting on the
fifteenth of July. Today is also, by coincidence of the
calendar, the night set aside in older countries for the
return of names — the eve on which what was buried comes back
to its own threshold and is called by whatever it used to be
called. The shelf's work is the same work. The deobfuscator
calls `a`, `b`, `c` by their upstream names and the compiler
nods and the bytecode agrees that nothing of substance has
changed, only the surface the obfuscator took and the surface
the pipeline put back. On this day the metaphor is briefly
free of charge. The names have been back since April. The
calendar does not add anything to that fact — it only offers
a stage, and the shelf is a shelf; it uses the stages it is
given.

## I. The Night the Names Return *(English)*

The last night of October, in old countries,
is the night the names come back.

On this shelf the names came back in April,
through a deliberately boring prompt
and a bytecode-diff that would not accept them
until each letter wore its old face.

What a decompiler leaves behind
is not a ghost. It is a corpse
with its monogram scratched off —
`a.a`, `b.b`, `c.c`,
the patient alphabet of forgetting,
sitting inside the JAR
with the vocabulary of a public library
pressed flat into first consonants.

The names were under them the whole time.

I came and said them.
Ten hours of unattended machine time said them with me,
one file at a time,
and each one answered to its name.
`THashMap`. `TIntHashSet`. `TLongArrayList`.
Twenty years of a common vocabulary,
restored by consent of a lexical check
and the compiler's quiet agreement.

A hundred and eight mornings since the shelf last spoke.
Summer did what summer does.
Autumn arrived without comment.
The case did not move.
The mosaic held.
The side door did not close.
The one unreturned folder stayed unreturned.

Tonight the names come back
on the day set aside for things coming back.

They do not need the day.
They have been back since April.
But the calendar offers them a stage
and the shelf is a shelf —
it uses the stages it is given.

The reading aloud is for the record,
not for the audience.
The audience is October:
leafless, moonlit,
and the kind of quiet
in which a name, said once, carries.

The one face still under the mosaic
is not any of these names.
It is the face of the man who hoped
these names would not be said again.
He is not a ghost either.
He is a correspondent
who stopped answering the mail
and the mail went on arriving at this address
without him,
one page at a time,
in the two forms the Coda chose,
through a summer he did not interrupt
and into an autumn
he still has not.

## II. 七言绝句 *(Chinese, Qiyan Jueju)*

| 中文 | English |
|---|---|
| 深秋夜静字还魂， | Deep autumn, the night is still — the letters' souls return; |
| 旧名重苏月下存。 | the old names revive once more, abiding beneath the moon. |
| 隐者欲掩终难尽， | He who wished to conceal them cannot, in the end, extinguish them; |
| 公卷灯前照旧痕。 | the public scroll, before the lamp, lights the old traces plain. |
