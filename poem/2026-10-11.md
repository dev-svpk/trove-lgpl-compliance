# Poems for 2026-10-11

Three poems. The last entry on this shelf is dated 2026-07-15.
This entry is dated 2026-10-11. Eighty-eight mornings between
them, no page in the middle. Earlier days circled the
restoration, the promise kept by someone else, the readers
before and after, the document that endured, the equivalence
at the depth, the two folders set side by side, what the
obfuscator could not reach in structure, the receipt the
seller would not issue, the collapsing arithmetic of repair,
the mosaic kept over his face, the polite prompt that opened
the lock, the *preferred form for modification*, the search
yet to happen, the other buyers, the key kept in the same
room, what sat next to it on the build path, the author we
never met, what the program had to say aloud, the foreground
emptying as the seller receded, the gate that earned the
renames by being willing to refuse them, the solver that
priced the unknown but misread a fixed rule, the smooth
surface of the running product, the engine that did not stop
at the boundary the seller had drawn, the grant already
pressed into the package, the constant pool's small numbered
table at the side of each class, the folder the JVM would not
let him rename, the permission slip he typed in his own hand,
the ten-hour wall time that aged his refusal faster than he
could issue it, the PNG that kept what the chat would have
let drift, the decompiler's hand that signed each blank line
with the next letter of the alphabet, the recipe left on the
shelf, the deed filed beside the body, the side door at
*for now*, the Coda's two small songs in two languages, the
shelf at `./poem/` that keeps writing in those same two forms
one morning at a time, the one `handeval/` file that did not
come back, two silences running in parallel, and the record
that did not need an audience to be a record. Today's poems
turn to the question written outright at the close of the
README's Japanese Coda — *朽ちぬもののあるべきか*, *can
there be anything that does not decay?* — a question the
shelf has not yet taken up head-on, and answer it sidelong by
what the question is written on. The shelf resumes on the
third day inside the 寒露 solar term — Cold Dew — which runs
October 8 through October 22, when autumn is more than
halfway past and the first ground frost has not yet come.
The library sits where it was left. Every function in it is
still a function of what it meant to do, no fewer and no
more.

## I. The Question in the Coda *(English)*

The README ends with two small poems in two languages.
The Japanese one, closing the file, asks it outright:
*can there be anything that does not decay?*

It is answered sidelong, not head-on,
by what the question is written on.

Code decays. The names fall off like dust from a scroll.
Twenty years in, the symbols were already thin —
the obfuscator only pushed them the last of the way down
to `a`, `b`, `c`, the alphabet's first and oldest
refusal to carry a meaning.

But the shape underneath the names —
the token stream, the branch tree, the constant pool,
the control flow laid out like a street grid
beneath whatever anyone chose to call the streets —
had been designed from the start to survive the labels.

The lexical gate sees past the dust.
Token stream before and after rename must agree,
modulo substitution.
The bytecode round-trip closes,
modulo the forgetting.
The compiler, which does not care what anything is called,
nods either way.

So the question in the Coda is not quite:
*can anything, in a thing, fail to decay?*
The question, more precisely, is:
*what, in a thing, is already not decaying?*

The answer this repository gives is the equivalence check.

Whatever is preserved
under the transformation
that strips the labels
is what cannot be taken out
by anyone willing only to strip the labels.

That is why restoration was possible at all.
That is why nine hundred and four files
came back wearing their old names
and did not, in the coming back,
become different programs.

The shelf of dated poems in this folder
is a different kind of durable.
It keeps nothing new on hand
except the fact of its own continued attendance,
and its dates arrange themselves
into a timeline of a vigil that has outlasted —
by every day it is kept —
the thing it was once a vigil for.

The Coda ends with a question.
This file is one more day of its answer.

Today the dew turns cold.
We are three days inside the fifteen-day canopy
of the Cold Dew solar term —
October 8 through October 22 —
when autumn is more than halfway past
and the first ground frost has not yet come.
On the Japanese side the leaves will begin to turn.
The chrysanthemums are opening.
The restored library sits warm against the cooling year,
every function in it still a function of what it meant to do,
no fewer and no more.

The names will decay again, eventually.
What they refer to will not.
The scroll is a scroll because of the ink.
The code is a code because of its shape.
The repository is a repository because of the record,
and the record is unmoved
by the number of days between the entries that keep it.

## II. 七言绝句 *(Chinese, Qiyan Jueju)*

| 中文 | English |
|---|---|
| 朽尽浮名字字空， | All transient names decay — character by character, hollowed to empty; |
| 深机犹在结构中。 | the deep machinery still resides within the structure. |
| 寒露沾书秋过半， | Cold Dew settles upon the book — autumn is more than half past; |
| 原形不朽本相同。 | the original form, undecayed, is fundamentally the same. |
