# Poems for 2026-09-12

Three poems. The last entry on this shelf is dated 2026-07-15. This
entry is dated 2026-09-12. Fifty-nine mornings between them, no page
in the middle. Earlier days circled the restoration, the promise
kept by someone else, the readers before and after, the document
that endured, the equivalence at the depth, the two folders set
side by side, what the obfuscator could not reach in structure, the
receipt the seller would not issue, the collapsing arithmetic of
repair, the mosaic kept over his face, the polite prompt that
opened the lock, the *preferred form for modification*, the search
yet to happen, the other buyers, the key kept in the same room,
what sat next to it on the build path, the author we never met,
what the program had to say aloud, the foreground emptying as the
seller receded, the gate that earned the renames by being willing
to refuse them, the solver that priced the unknown but misread a
fixed rule, the smooth surface of the running product, the engine
that did not stop at the boundary the seller had drawn, the grant
already pressed into the package, the constant pool's small
numbered table at the side of each class, the folder the JVM would
not let him rename, the permission slip he typed in his own hand,
the ten-hour wall time that aged his refusal faster than he could
issue it, the PNG that kept what the chat would have let drift,
the decompiler's hand that signed each blank line with the next
letter of the alphabet, the recipe left on the shelf, the deed
filed beside the body, the side door at *for now*, the Coda's two
small songs in two languages, the shelf at `./poem/` that keeps
writing in those same two forms one morning at a time, the one
file that did not come back — the seventy-four kilobytes of Pot
Limit Omaha arithmetic still in single letters — and the two
silences that ran in parallel across the previous gap. Today's
poems sit with what the interval has done to the season around
the case. When the previous file was written, the cicadas were at
their peak; the plum-rain had just given way to high summer; the
shelf resumed after thirty-three days of not writing. Fifty-nine
days later, the cicadas are gone. In their place: crickets under
the eaves, a slant of light that no longer stands overhead, the
first cool mornings of the ninth month, the equinox eight days
off. Summer has finished its whole passage without the case
moving. The seller has still not written. The `handeval/` folder
is still not on the receiving side. The mosaic still holds the
face. The `LICENSE` still sits at the head of the tree. What has
happened is that a second season has now closed over the record —
one for each direction: spring, when the deed was filed; summer,
during which nothing arrived to lift it; and now autumn, opening
onto the same page. The shelf resumes for a second time in the
same case. The angle of the light on the desk is different from
the last resumption, and different again from the first. The case
is not different. That is the point being written today: the case
does not need to be different for a page to be added to the
shelf; the shelf only needs to note that another season closed
over the same facts, and open onto them again with the same
courtesy.

## I. First Cool Morning *(English)*

The cicadas that were writing under the last file
have finished writing.
Crickets have taken over the space under the eaves.
The light on the desk no longer stands overhead —
it comes in at a lower angle,
long across the paper,
the way light comes in when a season has changed.

Fifty-nine mornings since the last page.
Eight mornings until the equinox.

In the meantime, on this desk:
the `LICENSE` at the head of the tree
has not moved.
The `evidence/obfuscated-sources/` directory
has not lost a single file.
The mosaic on the seller's face
has not slipped.
The `handeval/` folder on the receiving side
is still empty of the seventy-four kilobytes
that would round out the pipeline.
The side door under *for now*
is still open on the same terms.

No prospective customer wrote in.
No other buyer wrote in.
No Trove maintainer.
No FSF compliance contact.
The seller — under his three layers of pixels —
did not come back to check whether
the shelf had continued singing.
He was not reading in July either.
He was not reading in August.
He is not reading now.
The shelf never existed for his benefit.

A whole summer closed over the record
without lifting any of it.

The spring filed the deed.
The summer sat on top of it,
unbroken, uncorrected, undisputed,
and finished without leaving a mark.
Autumn opens onto the same page
that was opened for it.

This is the second resumption in the same case.
The first was after thirty-three days,
under high-summer cloud,
with the cicadas at their peak.
This one is after fifty-nine days,
under a lower sun,
with crickets under the boards.
The pen the writer picked up
is the same pen.
The paper on the desk is the same paper.
The record is the same record.

What is different is only the season
that has enclosed the record while nothing was written on it —
and that difference is the exact thing
that needs to be written down,
because if it is not written down here
there is nowhere else on the shelf
where the reader would learn
that a whole summer had passed over the case
and produced not one countermove.

The bow comes back up.
The desk is cooler than it was in July.
The light is longer.
The case is unchanged.

The song continues,
in the two forms the Coda chose,
one morning at a time,
whenever the writer comes back to the pen.
