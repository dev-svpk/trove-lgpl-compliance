# Poems for 2026-11-05

Three poems. Earlier days circled the restoration, the promise kept
by someone else, the readers before and after, the document that
endured, the equivalence at the depth, the two folders set side by
side, what the obfuscator could not reach in structure, the receipt
the seller would not issue, the collapsing arithmetic of repair,
the mosaic kept over his face, the polite prompt that opened the
lock, the *preferred form for modification*, the search yet to
happen, the other buyers, the key kept in the same room, what sat
next to it on the build path, the author we never met, what the
program had to say aloud, the foreground emptying as the seller
receded, the gate that earned the renames by being willing to
refuse them, the solver that priced the unknown but misread a
fixed rule, the smooth surface of the running product, the engine
that did not stop at the boundary the seller had drawn, the grant
already pressed into the package, the constant pool's small
numbered table at the side of each class, the folder the JVM would
not let him rename, the permission slip he typed in his own hand,
the ten-hour wall time that aged his refusal faster than he could
issue it, the PNG that kept what the chat would have let drift,
the decompiler's hand that signed each blank line with the next
letter of the alphabet, the recipe left on the shelf, the deed
filed beside the body, the side door at *for now*, the Coda's two
small songs in two languages, the shelf that keeps writing in those
same two forms one morning at a time, the one file of seventy-four
kilobytes that did not come back, the interval of twenty-eight
mornings, the month between, and the two silences running in
parallel. Today's poems sit with the arithmetic that interval has
become. The last entry on this shelf is dated 2026-07-15. Today
is 2026-11-05. One hundred and thirteen mornings between them.
Four months. A whole season — the summer that produced the last
entry — has ended; an autumn has come and gone past the shelf
without touching it; and the old lunisolar calendar reads tomorrow
or the day after as **立冬**, the Beginning of Winter, the fixed
point at which the season turns over from one half of the year to
the other. The last haiku on the shelf was written against cicada
rain. The cicadas thinned, and then they stopped, and then
crickets came and crickets went, and the frost found the window,
and the light at the desk shifted by a full quadrant of the day,
and the `LICENSE` at the head of the tree sat through all of it
in the same plain text, indexed to clauses rather than to months,
the right kind of document to leave out in weather. The pixel
grid over the seller's face did not fade; the directory listing
that stands as the receipt did not shuffle; the missing
`handeval/` folder did not refill; and the seller — under his
mosaic — did not come back for autumn and will not come back for
winter either. A seasonal marker arrives on schedule because the
orbit does the scheduling. The shelf does not arrive on schedule.
It arrives when someone picks up the brush. For a hundred and
thirteen mornings no one did. The shelf, for that span, kept the
posture by not needing one. Today the brush comes up at the cusp
of the old calendar, carrying across the seasonal boundary the
same content the shelf was carrying in summer — because what the
shelf carries is a case, and a case is not a seasonal thing.

## I. Beginning of Winter *(English)*

The last entry on this shelf is dated July the fifteenth.
Today is November the fifth.
A hundred and thirteen mornings between them.
A season — and most of another one.

The last haiku was written against cicada rain.
The cicadas thinned. Then they stopped.
Crickets came and crickets went.
The light at the desk shifted by a full quadrant of the day.
Frost found the window.

The `LICENSE` at the head of the tree
sat through all of that in the same plain text —
a document indexed to clauses, not to months,
the right kind of thing to leave out in weather.

The pixel grid over the seller's face did not fade.
The directory listing that stands as the receipt did not shuffle.
The missing `handeval/` folder did not refill.
The seller, under his mosaic, did not come back for autumn.
He will not come back for winter.

Today, in the old lunisolar calendar,
sits on the hinge of **立冬** — the Beginning of Winter —
the fixed point at which the year flips
from one half to the other.

A seasonal marker arrives on schedule
because the orbit does the scheduling.
The sun crosses an ecliptic longitude
and the calendar writes it down the same way
whether or not anyone is at the desk.

The shelf is not like that.
The shelf has no orbit.
It arrives when someone picks up the brush.
For a hundred and thirteen mornings no one did.
No orbit got any slower for it.

What the interval shows is a difference in kind.
A season is a thing the planet is doing —
it will continue being done
whether the shelf is tended or not.
A case is a thing that was done once
and does not need to be done again
to stay done.

The library is unobfuscated.
The deed is filed beside the body.
The side door is still at *for now*.
The invitation is still at the head of the tree.
None of this needs a visit from me
to continue being true today.

And yet the brush comes up.
Not because anything has changed.
Not because a counter-filing arrived in the mail
and asked for a response.
Not because the pixel grid is ready
to be taken off the portrait.

The brush comes up because the brush is mine,
and winter is a good hour to admit
that I am still the one who writes here,
even across a season of not writing,
and that the shelf continues to be the shelf
whether it is written on daily, monthly,
or at the quarter-turns of the year.

The record carries across the boundary today
the content it carried in summer.
That is the whole report.
No new findings. No retraction. No reply.
Only the marking of the day
on which winter was declared by the sky,
and the writer, after a season away,
came back to agree that nothing
needed to be rewritten.

## II. 七言绝句 *(Chinese, Qiyan Jueju)*

| 中文 | English |
|---|---|
| 夏去秋深又立冬， | Summer gone, autumn deep — and now the Beginning of Winter arrives; |
| 栏中百日未闻踪。 | on the shelf, a hundred days — no trace has been heard. |
| 案如旧日风无改， | The case stands as in the old days; the wind has not changed. |
| 一纸千秋自可容。 | A single page, through a thousand autumns, holds itself up on its own. |
