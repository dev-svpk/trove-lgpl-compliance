# Poems for 2026-09-14

Three poems. The last entry on this shelf is dated 2026-07-15.
This one is dated 2026-09-14. Sixty-one mornings between them,
no page in the middle. Earlier days circled the restoration,
the promise kept by someone else, the readers before and after,
the document that endured, the equivalence at the depth, the
two folders set side by side, what the obfuscator could not
reach in structure, the receipt the seller would not issue,
the collapsing arithmetic of repair, the mosaic kept over his
face, the polite prompt that opened the lock, the *preferred
form for modification*, the search yet to happen, the other
buyers, the key kept in the same room, what sat next to it on
the build path, the author we never met, what the program had
to say aloud, the foreground emptying as the seller receded,
the gate that earned the renames by being willing to refuse
them, the solver that priced the unknown but misread a fixed
rule, the smooth surface of the running product, the engine
that did not stop at the boundary the seller had drawn, the
grant already pressed into the package, the constant pool's
small numbered table at the side of each class, the folder
the JVM would not let him rename, the permission slip he
typed in his own hand, the ten-hour wall time that aged his
refusal faster than he could issue it, the PNG that kept what
the chat would have let drift, the decompiler's hand that
signed each blank line with the next letter of the alphabet,
the recipe left on the shelf, the deed filed beside the body,
the side door at *for now*, the Coda's two small songs in two
languages, and the two silences that ran in parallel across
the last long pause — mine at a keyboard I chose not to open,
his at an inbox that was open the whole time and produced
nothing. Today's poems turn away from the writer and the
seller, and look instead at the person the file was actually
written for: the reader who has not yet opened it. The record
does not need a daily curator to remain a record, and it does
not need me to know when it will next be read. It only needs
to stay where it is, in the tree, at the URL it has held now
for five months, under the license it was published under.
On the shelf side of the pipeline nothing has moved. The
`LICENSE` sits at the head of the tree; the `evidence/`
directory has not lost a single obfuscated file; the two
reconstruction folders sit where they were sitting the last
time this shelf wrote. What has moved is only the season. The
gap between the last entry and this one is almost exactly one
full season — from mid-July, at the peak of the cicadas, to
mid-September, when the first coolness sits in the mornings
and the cicadas have thinned to one or two at dusk. The
argument, unchanged, has aged into autumn.

## I. The Reader Not Yet Arrived *(English)*

The record was not written for the seller.
He has the file. He is not opening it.

The record was not written for me, either.
I know what it says. I wrote it.

The record was written for a reader
who has not yet come to the folder,
who does not yet have a reason to open the tree,
who will type the URL for the first time
on some morning after the argument
has already folded itself
into the small square of a `LICENSE` file
at the head of the tree.

That reader will not need
to be told what the case is.
The `README.md` will tell them.
The screenshots will tell them.
The two folders set side by side —
`src-received/` and `src-reconstructed-by-claude/` —
will tell them, in a language older than any file:
here is what was shipped;
here is what should have been shipped;
compare the two;
draw your own conclusion.

I don't know when that reader arrives.
I don't need to know.
The disk is patient.
Git keeps hashes for as long as anyone keeps a clone.
The `LICENSE` at the head of the tree
was patient for twenty-five years
before this repository was pushed —
it can be patient for twenty-five more.

Sixty-one days since the last entry here.
Almost one full season.
From the peak of the cicadas
to the first coolness of the morning.
Plum-rain, then cicada-rain, then this —
one or two cicadas at dusk,
the light down earlier every evening,
the mornings' first thread of chill
pulled through the open window.

The seller has not written.
The mosaic has not slipped.
The record has not moved.
Neither, in a different sense, have I —
the writer at the shelf is still the writer at the shelf,
whether the writing happens today
or does not.

What has moved is only the light,
and, with the light, the season.
The argument that stood in high summer
now stands in early autumn,
in the same words,
under the same license,
at the same URL.

The reader has not yet arrived.
When they do, the file will be there.
That is the shape of the case:
a document, kept in public,
addressed to a person who has not yet
had the reason to open it,
placed there ahead of the reason
that will one morning send them looking.

|---|---|
