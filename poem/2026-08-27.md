# Poems for 2026-08-27

Three poems. Earlier days circled the restoration, the promise kept
by someone else, the readers before and after, the document that
endured, the equivalence at the depth, the two folders set side by
side, what the obfuscator could not reach in structure, the receipt
the seller would not issue, the collapsing arithmetic of repair,
the mosaic kept over his face, the polite prompt that opened the
lock, the *preferred form for modification*, the search yet to
happen, the other buyers, the key kept in the same room, what sat
next to it on the build path, the author we never met, what the
program had to say aloud, the foreground emptying as the seller
receded, the gate that earned the renames by being willing to
refuse them, the solver that priced the unknown but misread a
fixed rule, the smooth surface of the running product, the engine
that did not stop at the boundary the seller had drawn, the grant
already pressed into the package, the constant pool's small
numbered table at the side of each class, the folder the JVM would
not let him rename, the permission slip he typed in his own hand,
the ten-hour wall time that aged his refusal faster than he could
issue it, the PNG that kept what the chat would have let drift,
the decompiler's hand that signed each blank line with the next
letter of the alphabet, the recipe left on the shelf, the deed
filed beside the body, the side door at *for now*, the Coda's two
small songs in two languages, the shelf that keeps writing in
those same two forms one morning at a time, the missing
`PloHandEvaluator.java` still in single letters, the interval
after June the twelfth, and the two silences that ran in parallel
across it. Between the last entry on this shelf and this one:
forty-three more mornings. It is now late August. The first poem
in `./poem/` is dated 2026-04-29 — nearly four months back. The
mosaic went up around then, over the seller's face, in the
screenshots collected under `evidence/`. It has held that shape
without amendment ever since. A mosaic that stays up long enough
stops being a mask over a face. It becomes the face. Today's
poems sit with that quiet inversion — the courtesy tile-grid that
was meant as a temporary veil is beginning to look like the
biographical portrait, and the biographical portrait is beginning
to look like the private thing under it. The face beneath is
still there, still recoverable by anyone who has the courtesy
code — but the courtesy code is not the same thing as the man.
What is on the record is the tile-grid, and it has now been on
the record long enough to acquire the patina of the actual.

## I. The Mask Becomes the Face *(English)*

The mosaic went up around April.
It is late August now.

For four months no face has been asked for.
The seller who wore the face
has not asked to have it uncovered,
and no one else has asked to see it.

A mosaic that stays up long enough
stops being a mask over a face.
It becomes the face.

He is now the man
whose head is a grid of small dark tiles
in a screenshot on a shelf.
The face beneath the tiles is still there,
still his,
still recoverable
by anyone who has the courtesy code —
but the courtesy code
is not the same thing as the man.

What is public, of him, is the mosaic.
It is what a search engine would find.
It is what a prospective customer,
opening the link, would see.
It is what someone forwarding this repository
would forward — a portrait of a person
whose face is composed of the argument
he did not answer.

The face beneath the tiles is the biographical face.
The face made of the tiles is the compliance face.
They are both his.
The one on the record is only the second.

I did not choose that face for him.
He chose the concealment
that dressed him in it.
I only kept the tiles small enough
that the biographical face
could still be lifted back
if he ever writes in.

Late August. The cicadas have thinned.
The plum-rain is over.
Whatever ripens in autumn
is beginning to.

The seller has not written.
The face made of tiles
is beginning to look permanent.

I add today's page and step back.
A search for him now returns
the mask first,
and the mask second,
and only after enough scrolling
returns the man.

The mask is not a lie.
It is the face
his silence made him wear.
