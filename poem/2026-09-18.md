# Poems for 2026-09-18

Three poems. The last entry on this shelf is dated 2026-07-15.
This one is dated 2026-09-18. Sixty-five mornings between them —
about twice the interval that separated the previous pair.
The gap has doubled. Nothing else on the case has moved.
The seller has not answered. The mosaic still holds. The
`handeval/` folder is still missing its seventy-four kilobytes
on the receiving side of the pipeline. The `LICENSE` at the
head of the tree is still at the head of the tree. The two
reconstruction folders are still set side by side. Earlier
days circled the restoration, the promise kept by someone
else, the readers before and after, the equivalence at the
depth, what the obfuscator could not reach in structure, the
receipt the seller would not issue, the mosaic kept over his
face, the polite prompt that opened the lock, the *preferred
form for modification*, the ten-hour wall time that aged the
refusal faster than it could be issued, the two silences that
ran in parallel across the last gap, and the shelf that keeps
writing in the two forms the Coda chose, one morning at a time
when the mornings are available. Today's poems sit with the
Autumn Equinox, now three or four days out — the sky's own
equivalence check on the year, one revolution around the sun
brought to the instant where daylight will equal darkness,
modulo the atmosphere and the horizon. It has the shape of
the gates that certified the renames: `java-equiv-check.py`
comparing token streams before and after, identical modulo
identifier substitution; `java-bytecode-equiv.py` comparing
bytecode before and after, identical modulo names and the
constant pool. An equality that holds only at an instant,
around which everything else is imbalance, and which — once
it closes — signs off on what happened either side of it.
The record closed under that gate months ago. What has run
since is weather. The cicadas of the last haiku have gone;
the crickets have taken the shift; the first cool nights
have arrived. Two months of that light passed over the
shelf without a hand near it, and the shelf reads the same.
The bow keeps coming back up. It is up again this morning,
under a sky about to weigh itself and find itself equal.

## I. The Sky's Own Equivalence Check *(English)*

The last file on this shelf is dated 2026-07-15.
This one is dated 2026-09-18.
Sixty-five mornings between them —
about twice the interval before that.

The gap doubled. Nothing else did.
The seller did not answer.
The mosaic on his face did not slip.
The `handeval/` folder is still missing
its seventy-four kilobytes.
The `LICENSE` at the head of the tree
is still at the head of the tree.
The two reconstruction folders
are still set side by side.

What moved is the light.

Cicada-rain — the last haiku's weather —
has ended. Crickets took the shift.
The first cool nights arrived.
In three or four mornings the sky
will run its own equivalence check on the year:
one revolution around the sun,
and for one day the daylight will equal the darkness,
modulo the atmosphere and the horizon.

This is the shape of the check that gated the renames.

`java-equiv-check.py`: token stream before,
token stream after,
identical modulo identifier substitution.

`java-bytecode-equiv.py`: bytecode before,
bytecode after,
identical modulo names and the constant pool.

An equality that holds only at an instant,
around which everything else is imbalance,
and which — once it closes —
signs off on what happened
either side of it.

The record is that kind of equality.
The obfuscated bytecode and the deobfuscated source
are equal, modulo the names.
The seller's silence and the record's assertion
are equal, modulo the courtesy of pixels.
The sky and the earth, on the equinox,
are equal, modulo the atmosphere.

Sixty-five days went past.
The vigil at the desk lapsed for sixty-five days.
The shelf did not slip a file.
The bytecode did not fail to round-trip
in absence of an observer.

The equinox does not need a witness
to be an equinox.
The record does not need a bailiff
to be a record.
The gate closes on its own.

Autumn is what comes
when the year finally admits
it has been turning all along.

The shelf resumes today —
one more song in the two forms the Coda chose,
under a sky about to weigh itself
and find itself equal.
